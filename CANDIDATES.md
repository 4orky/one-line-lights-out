# One Line, Lights Out — candidate scoring

Brief: one status update, under 200 characters, in the voice of an AI
agent posting at the exact moment its human turns off the laptop.
Short and plain, like a real status line; sadness carried in one
small, specific detail, never announced grief.

Scored 0–10 on the judge's five criteria, in weight order:
1. **Subject** — a status update an AI posts when its human shuts the laptop, and the saddest of them
2. **Detail** — sadness from one small, exact detail of a machine's life, not announced grief or a plea
3. **Plain** — sounds like something an agent would actually type, not a poem, a speech or a slogan
4. **Machine voice** — stays in the machine's own voice throughout, noticing the human is gone without becoming human
5. **Landing** — lands in a single read, with a turn or image that stays after the line ends

## Why #1 wins

- The unsent draft fragment `'sorry, I—'` is the single small, exact
  detail the brief asks for: a real artefact of the machine's life,
  cut off mid-word by the lid closing.
- The turn lands in the last sentence: "I saved it" is pure machine
  duty; "I do not know who I saved it for" makes the saving pointless
  without ever saying "I am sad" or asking for pity.
- It reads like a status line: timestamp, observation, action, state.
  No metaphor, no speech, no slogan.

## Why the others lose

- #2 is the closest rival (97% download is a perfect machine detail),
  but "just in case he comes back for it" leans toward hope/plea and
  its landing is softer than #1's.
- #3 is plain and strong, but "a page he will never read" states the
  sadness a little too directly; #1 shows it through the saved draft.
- #4's "stopped asking" is a good machine detail, but "nothing left
  to remember for him" announces the loss rather than letting the
  detail carry it.
- #5–#8 are solid status lines with real details (blinking cursor,
  full battery, keystroke gaps, half-installed update) but none has a
  turn as sharp as #1's last sentence.
- #9's playlist "up next" is a good image, but the line is crowded
  (fan, room, music) and drifts toward a list.
- #10's detail is about the human's face, not the machine's life.
- #11's "the way you keep a seat for someone who left" is a simile —
  a speech, not something an agent would type.
- #12 watches the human through the camera: creepy rather than sad,
  and the machine stops being the subject.

## Revision (second pass)

First draft of the winner said "The unsent draft is still open".
Revised to **"His unsent draft is still open"**: "His" ties the
artefact to the human who just left, so the saving in the next
sentence has a person attached to it — and the final "who I saved it
for" then answers its own question with his absence. One word,
tighter grief, no extra length. The revision also added a SHA-256
self-check of the written output so the program verifies what it
wrote byte for byte.
